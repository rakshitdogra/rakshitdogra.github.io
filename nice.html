Forecasting Vulnerability Surges in Open-Source Packages

Which packages will see a burst of high or critical advisories in the next 30–180 days, and can we explain why, without fooling ourselves with data leakage?

4 research questions 7 baselines 3 horizons 12 weeks
The idea

Why package-level surges matter

Disclosures cluster in time. A burst forces downstream teams into repeated triage, patching and testing. Existing tools score single CVEs or forecast ecosystem totals; package-level burst forecasting under leakage-controlled, cost-aware evaluation looks under-studied (literature check pending).

🎯 The task

For package i at date t , estimate the probability of a surge in (t, t+h], using only information public at t .

⚠️ The caveat

We forecast disclosure surges, not true vulnerability density. Audits, fuzzing or policy changes can also trigger bursts.

🧪 The paper type

Empirical study plus benchmark: leakage audit, budget-aware cost analysis, and explanation stability.

Hypotheses

Four research questions

RQ1 · How much do naive evaluations overstate performance?

Hypothesis: random splits and today's metadata inflate results. Falsified if there is no meaningful gap. Analysis: same models under each protocol, PR-AUC gap with confidence intervals.

RQ2 · What signal actually predicts surges?

Hypothesis: history dominates, but activity and dependency features add value at longer horizons. Falsified if nothing beats a history-only model. Analysis: feature-family ablations, paired block bootstrap.

RQ3 · Does cost-sensitive decision-making help?

Hypothesis: thresholding calibrated probabilities matches or beats class weights, focal loss and resampling at a fixed review budget. Analysis: expected cost across ratios, precision@k and recall@k.

RQ4 · Are explanations stable?

Hypothesis: rankings shift across time and cost settings, most around ecosystem-wide events. Analysis: rank correlation, top-k overlap, deletion-test faithfulness, explanation drift, case studies.
